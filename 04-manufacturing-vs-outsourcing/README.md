# Manufacturing vs. Outsourcing: Engineering Cost Analysis

**Tools:** Microsoft Excel  
**Project Type:** Manufacturing Economics and Financial Decision Analysis  
**Course:** MECH 408 – Applied Engineering Economy  
**Completed:** Spring 2025 (Individual Project)

## Project Overview

This project evaluates whether a hypothetical electric vehicle manufacturer should produce electronic control units (ECUs) domestically or purchase them from an overseas supplier.

The analysis compares the financial performance of both sourcing strategies over a ten-year period.

The objective was to evaluate how initial investment, manufacturing costs, tariffs, depreciation, taxes, and cash flows influence the sourcing decision.

## Business Problem

A hypothetical manufacturer, Voltara, requires 50,000 ECUs annually for ten years.

Two alternatives were considered.

### Alternative A: Domestic Manufacturing

- Initial investment: $8,000,000
- Annual operating expenses: $2,500,000
- Estimated salvage value: $500,000
- Depreciation: MACRS

### Alternative B: Overseas Outsourcing

- Initial investment: $200,000
- Supplier cost: $33.30 per ECU
- Import tariff: 150%
- Annual shipping and handling: $300,000
- Depreciation: Straight-line over five years

Both alternatives were evaluated using a 21% corporate tax rate and a 15% minimum acceptable rate of return (MARR).

## Methodology

An Excel model was developed to calculate annual financial performance over ten years.

The analysis included:

- Initial capital investment
- Annual revenue and operating costs
- Depreciation
- Taxable income and taxes
- Before-tax cash flow
- After-tax cash flow
- Net present value (NPV)
- Internal rate of return (IRR)
- Incremental financial comparisons

Nonfinancial factors, including supply chain reliability, quality control, and intellectual property protection, were also considered.

## Original Findings

The original analysis identified domestic manufacturing as the preferred sourcing alternative under the assignment's assumptions.

The report calculated an incremental NPV of approximately $1.78 million in favor of domestic manufacturing.

## Limitations

The model is based on a hypothetical manufacturing scenario with fixed cost, tariff, production, and revenue assumptions.

Real-world sourcing decisions would require additional analysis of changing market conditions, production capacity, supply chain risks, and operational constraints.
